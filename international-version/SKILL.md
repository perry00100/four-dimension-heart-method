# Four Dimensional Mind Framework

## Philosophy

**Origin**: Western adaptation of 四维心法 (Four Dimensional Heart Method) by 子夜

**Core Principle**: Human decision-making operates across four interdependent dimensions. Mastery comes not from controlling outcomes, but from harmonizing these dimensions with situational reality.

---

## The Four Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Foundation (根本) — Being
*Greek: Φύσις (Physis) | Stoic: Nature*

The unchanging core. Before action, establish your true position. What remains constant regardless of external circumstances?

**Key Questions**:
- What is my actual current state? (not what I wish it to be)
- What constraints cannot be negotiated?
- What do I genuinely want vs. what do I think I should want?

**Practice**: Daily grounding check-in, separate facts from interpretations.

---

### Dimension 2: Flow (上善) — Adaptation
* Taoist: 无为 (Wu Wei) | Process Philosophy: Becoming*

Optimal action without force. Like water finding its path, effective action works with reality's grain rather than against it.

**Key Questions**:
- What is the natural direction of this situation?
- Where is the path of least resistance?
- What would "effortless effort" look like here?

**Practice**: Observe patterns before acting. Wait for the gap between impulse and response.

---

### Dimension 3: Structure (仁恕) — Relationship
*Confucian: 仁恕 | Virtue Ethics: Character*

How your actions affect others and your character. Intentions matter, but patterns of behavior define us.

**Key Questions**:
- Would I be comfortable if everyone knew I took this action?
- What pattern am I establishing?
- Am I treating this person as an end, not merely a means?

**Practice**: Check your "legacy test" — does this action add to or subtract from who you want to become?

---

### Dimension 4: Balance (中庸) — Integration
*Aristotelian: Golden Mean | Dialectic: Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis*

The middle path between extremes. Neither too much nor too little. Timing and degree matter as much as direction.

**Key Questions**:
- Am I overcorrecting from a previous imbalance?
- What would "just right" look like?
- When is the right moment to act — not just what to do?

**Practice**: Pause at the threshold. Ask "Am I acting from fear or from wisdom?"

---

## Decision Protocol

When facing a choice:

```
1. FOUNDATION: What is actually true right now?
   ↓
2. FLOW: What wants to happen naturally?
   ↓
3. STRUCTURE: What kind of person does this make me?
   ↓
4. BALANCE: Is this proportionate to the situation?
   ↓
5. DECIDE: Act from integrated understanding
```
